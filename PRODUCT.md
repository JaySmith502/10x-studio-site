# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Business owners, operators, founders, franchise groups and leadership teams at mid-market companies with real operational complexity: multi-location service businesses, retailers selling online, teams running critical work out of spreadsheets and shared inboxes. Most are not technical. They arrive with a vague need ("we need a portal", "we need AI but don't know where it fits", "spreadsheets are holding the business together") and are deciding whether 10x Studio is safe to trust with a $25K to $500K build. Typical scene: an operations VP on a laptop in a bright office between meetings, comparing us against a freelancer and an agency.

## Product Purpose

10xstudio.dev sells 10x Studio as a senior build partner for custom web platforms, ecommerce systems, internal tools, AI-enabled workflows and integrations. The site has to translate vague needs into clear solution categories, prove capability through real case studies, and move a qualified buyer to a project conversation. Success is a serious buyer leaving with the sense that this team understands operations, can build, knows where AI actually fits, and will not sell vaporware.

## Brand Personality

Senior, exact, unhurried. Confident without flash; the confidence of people who have shipped the thing before. Voice is declarative, blunt and engineering-literate, addresses the client as "you" and speaks as "we" (one accountable team). Specific numbers earn their place; round numbers and hedges ("we think", "we might") do not. Emotional goal: relief, the buyer's sense that someone finally understands the mess and can turn it into a system.

Banned phrases: "digital transformation", "unlock your potential", "next-gen", "AI-powered everything", "Book a free consultation", "Get started now", "innovative synergistic platforms".

## Anti-references

- The previous 10x Studio look: Bauhaus primaries, hard ink borders, offset block shadows, circle-in-square motif. It reads as a neo-brutalist template and feels toy-like next to a six-figure platform conversation.
- Generic SaaS landing pages: centered hero, gradient blobs, icon-title-text card grids, hero-metric stat bands.
- Traditional dev-shop and agency sites: stock photos of people pointing at laptops, "digital transformation" copy, logo walls of clients they never had.
- Freelancer portfolios: dark mode plus neon, tech-stack badge walls.
- Startup cliché: dashboard mockups with fake charts, glassmorphism, sparkles for "AI".

## Design Principles

1. **Show the system, not the slogan.** Real work (case studies, real screens, real diagrams of how information moves) carries the argument. No decorative filler standing in for proof.
2. **Senior, not staffed.** Every surface should feel like one accountable team made deliberate choices. Restraint where it signals judgment, boldness where it signals conviction, nothing generic.
3. **Understandable, not intimidating.** A non-technical buyer must grasp what we build in one scroll. Technical depth is available, never required.
4. **Honest numbers only.** Metrics come from real builds or they don't appear. Anonymize clients rather than invent them.
5. **Practice what we preach.** The site itself is evidence of craft: fast, accessible, precise, with motion that explains rather than decorates.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. AA contrast on all text, including text on saturated color fields. Full keyboard support with visible focus. Every animation has a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback that keeps content static and complete. No information conveyed by color alone. The site stays usable without JavaScript.
